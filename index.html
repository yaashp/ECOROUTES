Everything you need to know about food miles & carbon tracking

Food miles refer to the distance food travels from where it is grown or produced to where it is finally consumed. The concept was coined by Professor Tim Lang in the 1990s to highlight the hidden environmental costs embedded in our food system.

Every time you eat an avocado flown in from Mexico, a kiwi shipped from New Zealand, or salmon airlifted from Norway, those kilometres carry a carbon cost. The longer and more emissions-intensive the journey, the larger the environmental footprint of that ingredient.

The FoodMiles calculator estimates the carbon footprint of each ingredient using three inputs: the origin of the food, your destination city, and the transport mode used to ship it.

  1. 01
    Select your city — Search from 500+ cities worldwide. This sets the destination coordinates for distance calculations.
  2. 02
    Add ingredients — Choose from 33+ pre-loaded foods, each with a known production origin (lat/lng) sourced from major growing regions.
  3. 03
    Set weight & transport — Enter how many kilograms you're using and select the transport mode. Each food has a smart default mode based on how it is typically shipped globally.
  4. 04
    See your impact — The calculator instantly computes distance, total CO₂ emissions, and renders colour-coded routes on an interactive world map.

Two formulas power every result in this calculator:

1 — Haversine Formula (great-circle distance)
a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁) × cos(lat₂) × sin²(Δlng/2)
distance = 2R × atan2(√a, √(1−a))
where R = 6,371 km (Earth's mean radius)

This calculates the shortest path between two points on Earth's curved surface — the true great-circle distance, as the crow (or cargo plane) flies.

2 — Emissions Formula (DEFRA methodology)
CO₂ (kg) = distance (km) × weight (kg) ÷ 1000 × carbon factor
Carbon factors sourced from DEFRA (UK Dept. for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs)

Weight is divided by 1,000 to convert kg → tonnes, since carbon factors are expressed per tonne-kilometre. This is standard practice used by logistics companies and governments worldwide.

Example — Avocado from Mexico to Mumbai
Weight: 0.3 kg · Mode: Air freight · Distance: ~13,500 km
CO₂ = 13,500 × (0.3 ÷ 1,000) × 0.602 = 2.44 kg CO₂

CO₂ and other greenhouse gases trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, driving global warming and climate change. Food transport contributes to this, and understanding your meal's footprint helps you make smarter choices.

🌿
Low — under 1 kg CO₂
Comparable to charging a smartphone ~100 times. Typical for locally grown staples — rice, lentils, or coconuts from India.
⚡
Moderate — 1–5 kg CO₂
Equivalent to a 20–40 km car trip. Common with road-freighted produce from neighbouring regions or mixed transport chains.
🔥
High — above 5 kg CO₂
Equivalent to driving 50+ km in a petrol car. Almost entirely driven by air freight — e.g. salmon from Norway or blueberries from the USA.

The single biggest lever: avoid air-freighted food. Air transport emits up to 75× more CO₂ per tonne-km than sea freight. The Greener Alternatives panel in the calculator shows you exactly which ingredient swaps cut your footprint the most.
