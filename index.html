What Are Food Miles?

Understanding the hidden cost of your plate

🗺️
Food Miles Explained
Food miles refer to the total distance food travels from where it is grown or produced to where it is consumed. The concept was coined by Professor Tim Lang in the 1990s to highlight the hidden environmental cost embedded in our food system. The longer and more emissions-intensive the journey, the larger the carbon footprint of that ingredient.
📌 An avocado from Mexico travels ~13,500 km to Mumbai
🌡️
Why It Matters
Global food transport contributes approximately 6% of all greenhouse gas emissions. CO₂ and other greenhouse gases trap heat in Earth's atmosphere, accelerating climate change — rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and biodiversity loss. Every food choice you make either adds to or helps reduce this burden.
🌿 Food system = ~26% of global CO₂ emissions
⚡
Transport & CO₂ Impact
Not all transport is equal. Air freight emits up to 75× more CO₂ per tonne-km than sea shipping. A single kilogram of blueberries flown from the USA generates more carbon than the same weight of rice shipped by sea from across the globe. Choosing local and sea-shipped produce dramatically cuts your footprint.
✈️ Air freight: 0.602 kg CO₂ per tonne·km

How It Works

The science and formulas behind the calculator

🔢 The Formulas
Step 1: We compute the great-circle distance between origin and destination using the Haversine formula:
a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)·cos(lat₂)·sin²(Δlng/2)
d = 2R · atan2(√a, √(1−a))
R = 6,371 km (Earth's mean radius)
Step 2: We calculate CO₂ emissions using the DEFRA methodology:
CO₂ (kg) = distance (km) × weight (kg) ÷ 1000 × carbon factor
Weight ÷ 1000 converts kg → tonnes (industry standard)
Example: Avocado from Mexico (0.3 kg, air, 13,500 km)
CO₂ = 13,500 × (0.3 ÷ 1000) × 0.602 = 2.44 kg CO₂
📊 Emission Factors (per tonne·km)
These factors are sourced from DEFRA (UK Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs) — the same methodology used by governments and logistics companies worldwide.
Mode CO₂ Factor Relative Best Use

Eco-Friendly Tips

Simple swaps and habits to shrink your food miles footprint

🏪
Buy Local
Choose produce grown within your region or country. Locally sourced food travels a fraction of the distance, dramatically reducing transport emissions. Visit farmers' markets for the freshest, lowest-footprint options.
💡 Indian local produce: Rice, Lentils, Mangoes, Coconuts, Tomatoes
🌱
Eat Seasonally
Out-of-season produce is often flown in from distant climates or grown in energy-intensive greenhouses. Seasonal foods are naturally available nearby, requiring far less transportation and refrigeration.
💡 Summer: Mangoes, Watermelon · Winter: Carrots, Spinach, Guava
🚢
Choose Sea Over Air
When imports are necessary, sea-shipped products carry up to 75× less carbon than air-freighted equivalents. Check product labels for "shipped" vs "airfreighted" indicators — especially for fish, berries, and exotic fruits.
💡 Sea-shipped coffee from Brazil ≈ 0.09 kg CO₂ vs. 5+ kg by air
🌿
Plant-Based Swaps
Plant-based foods generally have a much lower carbon footprint than animal products. Swapping imported salmon for local lentils can cut your per-meal emissions by over 90%. Legumes like dal are both local and low-carbon.
💡 Replace imported quinoa → Rajgira (Amaranth), grown locally
📦
Reduce Food Waste
Every tonne of food that is wasted also wastes all the carbon emitted during its transport. Plan meals carefully, buy only what you need, and compost leftovers to close the loop and reduce your overall food system impact.
💡 India wastes ~67 million tonnes of food annually
🔄
Smart Substitutions
Many exotic superfoods have nutritional equivalents grown locally. Replace imported blueberries with Jamun, quinoa with Rajgira, olive oil with sesame oil, and almond milk with coconut milk — all grown closer to home.
💡 Jamun = same antioxidants as blueberries, grown in India
